Q1. WHAT PROBLEM DOES YOUR PROJECT SOLVE?
================================================================================

--- LONG ---

After online financial fraud in India, the first hour is what decides whether the
money comes back - a bank can freeze the beneficiary account before the balance
is moved onward. But cybercrime.gov.in merges two jobs with completely different
deadlines: the bank's, which is minutes, and the police investigation's, which is
weeks. It asks you to pick a category, register, and wait for an OTP - often
while the scammer is still on the phone - then complete the full complaint before
it accepts anything. The urgent half is trapped behind the slow one.

Golden Hour splits the report in two and sends the urgent half first: a nine-field
freeze packet, with no login, no OTP, and no required field. A freeze request is
never blocked for being incomplete. The police statement is collected afterwards,
unhurried, against the same acknowledgement number.

This is not a new idea. It is the sequence the government already runs on the 1930
helpline, which takes a short list of facts, issues an acknowledgement number, and
asks for the full complaint within 24 hours. The website just does not work that
way.

--- SHORT ---

India's cyber fraud portal makes victims pick a category, register, and wait for
an OTP - often while the scammer is still on the line - before it accepts
anything. It merges the bank's job, which takes minutes, with the police
investigation's, which takes weeks, and paces both at the slower one. Golden Hour
sends the bank's half first: nine fields, no login, no required field. The 1930
helpline already works this way; the website does not.

Q2. WHAT SPECIFIC FEATURE DID YOU BUILD OR IMPROVE FOR ROUND 2?
================================================================================

--- LONG ---

The rule that the model is not allowed to guess - and, for Round 2, the evidence
that it holds.

The rule already existed: every field the model reads is re-validated server-side,
and anything whose shape is wrong for the field it claims to be is marked
UNREADABLE rather than sent, however confident the model was. The stakes are
asymmetric, which is the whole reason: a missing transaction ID means a bank works
with what it has, but a wrong one means it freezes the wrong account while the
real one empties.

What was missing was proof. Round 2 added an extraction evaluation - 75 fields
across generated screenshots and text, of which 74 were correct, and 40 of 40 from
screenshots with nothing invented. That is the worst of three passes, not the
best. It also added a demo case where you can watch the server refuse a confident
wrong value, because until then every blank in the product came from the model
declining to read something, which demonstrates the prompt behaving well rather
than the guarantee actually holding.

One value still escaped: a dictated "fastcart dot pay at samplebank" came back as
a different but perfectly well-formed account, which shape checking cannot catch.
That is published on /honesty rather than left out.

--- SHORT ---

Server-side validation - the model may not guess. Every field is re-checked for
shape and marked UNREADABLE rather than sent wrong, because a missing transaction
ID is survivable and a wrong one freezes the wrong account. Round 2 added the
measurement: 75 fields, 74 correct, 40 of 40 from screenshots with nothing
invented (worst of three passes), plus a demo case where you can watch the server
refuse a confident misread. One value still escaped a shape check, and that is on
/honesty.
